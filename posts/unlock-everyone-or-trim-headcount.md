Cost reduction in an organisation is easily read as headcount reduction. So take two organisations. One unlocks everyone with AI. The other trims its headcount. Which one wins?

I think the first, and my reason is what the second one removes along with the people. Polanyi put it in 1966: "we can know more than we can tell." Much of what makes an organisation work is held that way. Which customer needs a call before the invoice goes out, why the process has that odd step, what the last outage taught the team. None of it is in a document, and the people who hold it often could not write it down if you asked.

A reduction takes that knowledge out of the door, and the organisation finds out which part it needed when something breaks. My reading is that AI does not close that gap. A tool can only work with what has been written down or handed to it, and nobody can hand over what they cannot say.

DORA's 2026 report on AI-assisted software development comes down on the same side. It recommends organisations not adopt a headcount-reduction strategy, and says retaining and training existing talent preserves institutional knowledge. That is a recommendation with reasons, not a measured comparison.

Unlocking everyone runs the other way. I would also argue that AI is inherently user friendly, so everyone who adopts it can add more value. The people who hold the knowledge are the ones using the tools, so they are also the ones who decide what goes into them. In my own work, each function contributes the skills it knows, and I turn those into plugins and connectors for the AI assistants. The people who do the work every day write them, so what goes in is their call. Some of the tacit knowledge becomes explicit that way, on the terms of the person who holds it.

How much of what your organisation knows has never been written down?
