Today I felt in control. I think I have reduced the cognitive load to a level where I can run several projects in parallel.

I am recording everything, but I still need to test it. The challenge is that each project runs at its own pace, set by how fast the agent works. What I have not solved is sequencing the work so that there are fewer handoffs. It feels like I am sprinting from handoff to handoff, and I cannot batch them together well enough.

It feels fast, but it is like climbing a mountain. Just as you reach the top of one hill, you discover another, and another.

I think I am probably not investing enough time in planning. What I do now is describe, as well as I can, what needs to happen at each handoff.

I still hit limits, and I need to pace myself. That is probably why I felt more in control today: I could not run at the same pace.

This is more an internal struggle than an external one. I need to manage up, sideways, and down, but most of all I need to manage myself.

There is a word for what I am after. Ataraxia is the Pyrrhonist's word for composure, for not being disturbed. I have visited a wine farm with that name in the Hemel-en-Aarde valley, near Hermanus. It is nestled between the valleys, overlooking its own little view of the world, and its building used to be a small chapel. It feels like I am aiming for ataraxia at pace.

Kaizen says there is always another hill. Ataraxia is not being disturbed by it.

The Pyrrhonists did not catch it by chasing it. It came once they stopped insisting that things must be a certain way. Maybe that is why today felt different. I could not run at the same pace, so I stopped trying to.
