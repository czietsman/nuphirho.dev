From June to September 2026 I merged about eleven times as many pull requests a month as my own average across 2021 to 2025. The step came in a single month: June.

So what changed? Two things at once. In January my role became one where building is the job again, and agentic tooling changed what building costs. A decade of my own record says the first matters more than people think.

That comes from a record of every pull request I merged at work from late 2016 to September 2026. It counts merged changes, nothing more.

In every year my role was hands-on engineering, I ranked in the top five. The one exception is the year I was a product manager, 2019, when my count fell to a small fraction of the usual. The data begins in late 2016, so it does not cover my earlier years.

My reading is that the job decided how much I built. The one year I was specifically not allowed to build is the one year the record drops. As a VP my monthly rate held at about the level of my engineering years, even though my meeting load had roughly doubled. Being busy with other work did not suppress the count. What the role was for did.

A count rewards working in small increments, so it is fair to ask whether the work was simply split finer. My typical change was smaller than the team's in several years, but across the decade my distribution of change sizes almost exactly matches the team's, so the high count reflects more changes, not the same work split finer.

The record shows when the 2026 step happened, not why. My own account, and the data cannot test it, is that AI use began in 2024 as code completion and copy and paste, and that agentic tooling in 2026 was a much bigger enabler.

I have been a professional developer for more than two decades, and I started programming in primary school. I built products and features that still underpin what we sell today. A count of what got built will still undercount a job whose purpose is something else.

If you counted merged changes for your own role across the same years, would the record follow the job you were asked to do? And if it shows you stopped building, how do you relate to how the industry is changing now?
