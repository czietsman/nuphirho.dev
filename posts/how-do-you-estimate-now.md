I have never been good at estimation. Estimating my own work is hard. Estimating someone else's is harder. And it scares me when someone asks me for one, because they never really ask for an estimate. It is always a veiled commitment.

What I can do is break work down. I know the steps, I can sequence them, and I see the big picture. The hard part was always the handoff: relying on other people to get something done.

With AI I can do more myself, but my upfront guess can still be wildly off. I recently got an analytics proof of concept working locally. It worked for me, I could see the results, and it was quick. The next step is making the local version accessible to everyone, and that involves so many people. The requirements pile up as more people get involved. That translates into more work: you either accept it or skip it, but skipping it does not remove the requirement.

The handoffs have not gone away either. I still need to get access to things, and I still have to coordinate. That part seems to have become even more challenging. I think it is because the work has condensed: the steps I can do myself have got shorter, so the waiting is a bigger share of the whole. And everyone I wait on is busier with their own work. I can't fault them. I am busier than ever too.

So I track the goal. Keeping focus on it is what keeps me on track, even when the estimate turns out wrong.

How do you estimate today?
