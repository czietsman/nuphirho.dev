For most of my career I have measured myself against my own past output. Today the comparison is a team of my former selves.

A count of merged changes is not a measure of value. It counts activity. Over a short span, for one person doing broadly the same work, it is still a useful signal.

I am merging roughly eleven times as many changes a month as my average across 2021 to 2025: about eleven of my former selves, working at once.

A jump like that deserves suspicion, so I checked three things. It is not rollouts counted many times over: changes repeated across repositories were at most 8% of a month, and 22% in August. The changes did not shrink to nothing: the typical change stayed in a similar size range to earlier in the year, and the share of tiny changes did not grow. And they landed: 92 to 97% of the changes I opened were merged, higher than earlier in the year.

My meeting hours roughly doubled over the same months. The output did not come from a quieter calendar.

The data shows when, not why. There were two steps. The first came when we moved to an AI coding plan billed by subscription. Use dipped when we briefly switched to an enterprise plan billed at API rates, and the second step came when we went back to subscription billing. My account of the cause is agentic coding, better processes around it, and deliberately optimising how I work.

The next step is the same question for the wider engineering team.
